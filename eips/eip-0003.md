# EIP-0003 — CRS Trust Scoring Standard

## Status

Draft

---

## Abstract

EIP-0003 defines the Continuity Reputation
Scoring (CRS) system for the BITY Autonomous
Node Token (ANT) Protocol.

CRS functions as the behavioral trust engine
of the ANT ecosystem.

The CRS system measures, verifies, accumulates,
decays, synchronizes, and evaluates protocol trust
across Ethereum, Solana, oracle systems,
autonomous execution systems, and future
supported protocol environments.

CRS is behavioral verification over time.

CRS is not ownership.

---

# Motivation

Traditional NFT systems measure ownership only.

ANTs require persistent behavioral verification
to support:

- Autonomous execution
- RAP evolution
- Oracle confidence weighting
- Dynamic augmentations
- Cross-chain trust continuity
- Reputation persistence
- Anti-spoofing protections
- Execution permissions
- Future AI-assisted coordination

Without a standardized trust system,
autonomous systems may become vulnerable to:

- Reputation farming
- Participation spoofing
- Oracle manipulation
- Artificial continuity inflation
- Execution abuse
- Cross-chain trust inconsistencies

CRS defines the canonical trust architecture
for ANT protocol behavior.

# Specification

# Core Principles

## 1. Trust Is Earned

CRS MUST accumulate through verifiable activity.

Examples include:

- Prediction participation
- Oracle-confirmed activity
- Driver continuity
- Braille participation
- Autonomous execution history
- Cross-chain continuity
- Long-term protocol participation
- Verified behavioral consistency

---

## 2. CRS Is Behavioral

CRS measures:

- Continuity
- Reliability
- Participation
- Verification consistency
- Trust persistence
- Historical integrity

CRS does NOT represent ownership value.

---

## 3. CRS Is Persistent

CRS SHOULD maintain historical continuity
across:

- Wallet changes
- Cross-chain synchronization
- Metadata updates
- RAP transitions
- Protocol upgrades

---

## 4. CRS Supports Decay

Inactive or malicious behavior MAY reduce CRS.

Decay MAY include:

- Inactivity decay
- Reputation degradation
- Oracle confidence reduction
- Participation expiration
- Trust penalties

---

## 5. CRS Supports Recovery

CRS systems SHOULD permit trust recovery
through renewed verified participation.

---

# CRS Components

## Continuity Score

Measures long-term participation consistency.

Factors MAY include:

- Consecutive participation
- Active epochs
- Session continuity
- Behavioral persistence
- Historical engagement

---

## Reputation Score

Measures verified trustworthiness.

Factors MAY include:

- Resolution accuracy
- Oracle confidence
- Verified execution history
- Reputation persistence
- Protocol integrity

---

## Participation Score

Measures verified ecosystem activity.

Examples:

- Prediction submissions
- Driver mode participation
- Braille interaction
- Oracle contributions
- Autonomous coordination

---

## Verification Score

Measures confidence in authenticity.

Factors MAY include:

- Oracle confirmations
- Multi-source verification
- Anti-spoofing analysis
- Cross-chain consistency
- Identity continuity

---

## Integrity Score

Measures protocol compliance and security behavior.

Factors MAY include:

- Malicious activity detection
- Replay resistance
- Oracle abuse prevention
- Spam resistance
- Behavioral anomaly analysis

---

# CRS Synchronization

CRS systems MUST support:

- Ethereum synchronization
- Solana synchronization
- Oracle-confirmed reconciliation
- Cross-chain continuity preservation
- Replay-resistant propagation

Cross-chain CRS divergence SHOULD trigger
reconciliation procedures.

---

# CRS and RAP

CRS MAY influence:

- RAP eligibility
- Augmentation permissions
- Proliferation permissions
- Autonomous execution permissions
- Agent coordination authority

Higher CRS MAY unlock expanded protocol behavior.

---

# CRS and Autonomous Execution

Autonomous execution MAY require:

- Minimum CRS thresholds
- Oracle-confirmed trust levels
- Historical continuity verification
- Integrity validation

ANTs with insufficient CRS SHOULD face
restricted execution permissions.

---

- Sybil attacks

- Artificial participation inflation

- Identity spoofing
- Oracle poisoning
- Cross-chain duplication

- Oracle confidence weighting
- Multi-source verification

- Auditable scoring procedures
